I am a Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence student at Cairo University (Expected Graduation: 2027), actively developing my career in defensive cybersecurity with a dedicated focus on SOC Analysis and Incident Response.

Rather than treating security as abstract theoretical concepts, I believe in an evidence-first approach: understanding exactly how an attack manifests inside packet captures, how rogue processes behave on endpoints, and how disparate event logs correlate to reveal an adversary's operational timeline.

My learning approach bridges rigorous foundational computer science principles—such as operating system internals, TCP/IP networking, and machine learning—with real-world security tools, hands-on packet inspection, and attack-defense simulations.

"I am fascinated by challenging investigations where the analyst must connect multiple fragmented pieces of evidence across network traffic and endpoint telemetry, rather than simply reacting to an automated alert."

Core Areas of Practical Focus

  • Attack Footprints in Telemetry

    Analyzing how threats, reconnaissance, and unauthorized communications appear in packet captures, DNS queries, and system event logs.

  • Endpoint & Process Triage

    Investigating parent-child process anomalies, suspicious PowerShell executions, unauthorized persistence, and living-off-the-land techniques.

  • IOC Extraction & Correlation

    Identifying Indicators of Compromise (hashes, malicious IPs, domain beacons) and correlating events across multiple sources to confirm intrusions.

  • Defensive Tool Operations

    Utilizing Wireshark, Snort IDS, Nmap, and Windows Event Viewer to evaluate detection rules, analyze traffic anomalies, and document findings.
